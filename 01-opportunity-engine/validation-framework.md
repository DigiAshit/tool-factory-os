# Validation Framework - Opportunity Engine

This document defines the qualitative criteria that every software opportunity must satisfy to pass validation.

## 1. The Validation-First Principle
Do not write code until demand is proven. 
- **Broadcast Stage**: Test the raw concept on socials.
- **The Unprompted Rule**: If at least 10 target users do not reach out unprompted (via DMs, comments, or emails) asking when it launches or expressing a burning need, the idea is considered dead. Kill it immediately.

## 2. Positioning: Different, Not Better
You cannot displace an established category leader by building a "better" version of their product. You will lose because they have a distribution moat.
- **Rule**: Position your product as fundamentally different, targeting a specific niche use case.
- **Case Study (Feather.so)**: Rather than trying to build a better WordPress or Ghost, Feather positioned itself strictly as the best "Notion to Blog" tool. It nailed that single workflow and achieved high MRR growth despite competition.
- **Heuristic**: If the value proposition is "Like [Leader], but with a better UI", reject it. It must be "For [Specific Group] who use [Specific Tool] and need [Specific Workflow]".

## 3. Product Scope: Complete Solution vs. Clever Feature
Avoid the trap of building a "feature pretending to be a product." This leads to initial signups but massive churn because the workflow feels incomplete.
- **Rule**: The product must solve a complete, end-to-end user workflow.
- **Case Study (Tweet Analytics email)**: Delivering Twitter analytics via daily emails reached $300 MRR quickly but stalled. Users did not want an email; they wanted a dashboard where they could actively analyze and write.
- **Heuristic**: If the user has to leave your tool to complete the immediate next step in their workflow, the solution is incomplete. Build the next step into the product.
